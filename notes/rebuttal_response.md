# Reviewer response

We thank the reviewers, and address both the empirical question (how recovery scales with Q-error) and the theoretical one (robustness of identifiability to nonuniform, off-distribution Q-error) with a strengthened theorem and new controlled experiments, all of which we will add to the paper.

**Recovery quality vs Q-estimation error.** We ran a new experiment to measure the Q-value error, and recovered WM error, across 20 training checkpoints of a MountainCar agent. As predicted by our theory, WM error decreases with Q-value error (Spearman $\rho = 0.83 \pm 0.03$), and the recovered model is consistently one to two orders of magnitude more accurate than the corresponding Q-function (at convergence, $\mathrm{WM}_{\mathrm{NMSE}} \approx 7\times10^{-3}$ vs $Q_{\mathrm{NMSE}} \approx 0.22$, ${\sim}31\times$).

**Robustness to nonuniform / off-distribution Q-error.** In short, the answer is positive: recovery holds under a distributional (on-support) bound rather than a uniform one. We both prove this and verify it experimentally.

*Theory.* Let $S_o = \bigcup_g \mathrm{Supp}(\pi_g)$ be the states the agent visits (pooled across goals) and $A_o(s) = \{\pi_g(s) : g\in\mathcal{G}\}$ the actions its policies take there. The reduced MDP over the pairs $\{(s,a) : s\in S_o,\, a\in A_o(s)\}$ is forward-invariant, and our finite-MDP recovery guarantees apply with $M$ and the approximation error restricted to $S_o$, needing only the on-support error $\varepsilon_o = \sup_{s\in S_o}\lVert Q(s,a,\cdot) - Q^\pi(s,a,\cdot)\rVert_1$. Two generalizations carry over: the stochastic pseudo-inverse gives $\lVert\hat{P}(s,a) - P(s,a)\rVert_1 \le \lVert M_{S_o}^{+}\rVert_1(1+\gamma m)\varepsilon_o$, and — matching our deterministic experiments — column-matching recovers $P(s,a)$ *exactly* once $\varepsilon_o < \mathrm{gap}(M_{S_o})/(2(1+\gamma m))$. Identifiability now requires only $|\mathcal{G}| \ge |S_o|$ rather than $|\mathcal{G}| \ge |\mathcal{S}|$. These are finite-MDP results; the continuous MountainCar task illustrates the same phenomenon, with continuous bounds a natural next step.

*Direct verification.* We inject a controlled error into the Q-values used for recovery and sweep its magnitude, placed off-support or on-support. For the estimator our theorem analyses — recovery confined to $S_o$, so the Bellman bootstrap never reads off-support Q — off-support error has **no effect at any magnitude**: on-support recovery is unchanged ($\mathrm{WM}_{\mathrm{NMSE}} \approx 4\times10^{-6}$) as injected off-support error grows from $0$ to $\mathrm{NMSE} \approx 2$, whereas the same error on-support degrades it by orders of magnitude (to $\approx 0.2$). The unconstrained world model inherits this robustness at realistic error, and is affected by off-support error only once it grows large enough to pull its predicted successors off $S_o$ — the boundary of our counterexample. *Corroboration (natural setting).* For the trained agent, the recovered model is $60\times \pm 7$ more accurate on its reachable set than uniformly; and while its distance to optimal degrades off-support ($\|Q - Q^{*}\|$ is $2.3\times \pm 0.2$ larger), the Bellman-residual error the bound actually depends on, $\|Q - Q^{\pi}\|$, is comparable on- and off-support ($1.2 \pm 0.2$) — i.e. not concentrated off-distribution.
