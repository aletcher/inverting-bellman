# Reviewer response

We thank the reviewers, and address both the empirical question (how recovery scales with Q-error) and the theoretical one (robustness of identifiability to nonuniform, off-distribution Q-error) with a strengthened theorem and new controlled experiments, all of which we will add to the paper.

**Recovery quality vs Q-estimation error.** We ran a new experiment to measure the Q-value error, and recovered WM error, across 20 training checkpoints of a MountainCar agent. As predicted by our theory, WM error decreases with Q-value error (Spearman $\rho = 0.83 \pm 0.03$), and the recovered model is consistently one to two orders of magnitude more accurate than the corresponding Q-function (at convergence, $\mathrm{WM}_{\mathrm{NMSE}} = 7\times10^{-3}$ vs $Q_{\mathrm{NMSE}} = 0.22$).


**Robustness to nonuniform / off-distribution Q-error.** In short, the answer is positive: recovery holds under a distributional (on-support) bound rather than a uniform one. We both prove this and verify it experimentally.

*Theory.* To generalise our results to on-support bounds, let $S_o$ be an agent's visited states (across all goals) and $A_o$ the actions it takes with non-zero probability on $S_o$. The reduced MDP over the pairs $S_o \times A_o$ is forward-invariant, and our guarantees apply verbatim by replacing $M$ with the matrix $M_{S_o}$ corresponding to the columns in $S_o$, and replacing the uniform error by the on-support error $\varepsilon_o = \sup_{s\in S_o}\lVert Q(s,a,\cdot) - Q^\pi(s,a,\cdot)\rVert_1$. In the stochastic case, Theorem 3 generalises to $\lVert\hat{P}(s,a) - P(s,a)\rVert_1 \le \lVert M_{S_o}^{+}\rVert_1(1+\gamma m)\varepsilon_o$, with unique recovery requiring only $|\mathcal{G}| \ge |S_o|$ instead of $|\mathcal{G}| \ge |\mathcal{S}|$. Similarly, in the deterministic case, Theorem 2 generalises to perfect recovery whenever $\varepsilon_o < \mathrm{gap}(M_{S_o})/(2(1+\gamma m))$. In other words, to answer the reviewer directly: $P$-learning still recovers a meaningful model under on-support bounds, but only on the states visited by the agent (as should be expected). While these bounds are for finite MDPs, the same phenomenon holds for continuous MDPs, as validated below.

*Experimental validation.* To check whether off-support error impacts on-support recovery, we inject artificial noise into a MountainCar agent's Q-function, either off-support or on-support. As predicted, off-support perturbation has 200x less effect on the WM ($\mathrm{WM}_{\mathrm{NMSE}} = 3\times10^{-6} \to 2\times10^{-5}$ as noise increases from $\mathrm{Q}_\mathrm{NMSE} = 0 \to 0.2$), compared with on-support error ($\mathrm{WM}_{\mathrm{NMSE}} = 3\times10^{-6} \to 4\times10^{-3}$). Moreover, if $P$-learning is modified to sample only states that the trained agent visits (which we did not do in the paper because we are interested in recovering a WM that is accurate across all states), on-support recovery is *completely* immune to off-support error ($\mathrm{WM}_{\mathrm{NMSE}} = 3\times10^{-6} \to 3\times10^{-6}$).
